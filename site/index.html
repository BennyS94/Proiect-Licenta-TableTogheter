Product story

Built for a shared household, not a single isolated diet.

Most meal planning starts from one person's needs. TableTogether starts from a household: several member profiles, different goals, dietary restrictions, preferences, cooking time constraints and one shared grocery reality.

The project focuses on a practical baseline: deterministic recipe selection, clear backend orchestration, curated data, and feedback that can be reused by later generations.

Application flow

From household profiles to meals, groceries and insights.

Create local accounts, household settings and member profiles with goals and preferences.

02

The backend sends profiles and targets to the Python generator for recipe selection.

03

Inspect recipes

Meals expose macros, cooking time, scaled ingredients and practical cooking steps.

04

Alternatives use a KNN-lite helper, preview validation and replacement before applying changes.

05

Shop once

The grocery list is aggregated from selected recipe ingredients and formatted for purchase.

06

Track progress

Saved daily snapshots feed the Insights page with target adherence and macro trends.

Profiles and planning

A household can contain several internal member profiles.

The mobile app lets the user move between members, inspect each profile's plan, and keep shared household behavior visible without turning the product into separate one-person planners.

Product demo

A short walkthrough of the mobile experience.

Cooking steps and alternatives

Recipe details stay practical, while replacements are validated before use.

Cooking sheets show ingredient amounts scaled to the generated portion. The Alternatives flow previews impact first, then applies replacements through the backend rather than directly mutating the visible card.

Groceries and progress

The generated plan becomes a shopping list and saved progress history.

Grocery output groups ingredients into readable categories with purchase suggestions and estimated prices. Insights use saved daily progress snapshots to show consumed totals, meal contribution and trends.

Project context

A thesis project designed as a clean, inspectable product baseline.

The project intentionally avoids inflated product claims: it is local-first, uses estimated prices rather than live store prices, and treats health-aware settings as non-clinical preferences.

Its main contribution is the end-to-end system shape: household profiles, backend-owned persistence, recipe-centered generation, grocery aggregation, feedback, alternatives and progress insights working together.
